# Task 03 — SOC Alert Triage: Final Report

## Objective

Review ten security alerts, document likely security significance and recommended response, and compare human and AI assessments where independently available.

## Scope and method

The entire dataset is **synthetic** and was created for a training exercise. No production system or customer information was queried. The analyst independently reviewed **Alerts 01 and 02** and recorded verdicts before seeing the corresponding AI assessments. The remaining eight alert reviews were generated by ChatGPT at the analyst's request; there are **no independent analyst verdicts** for Alerts 03–10.

The AI evaluated each provided alert summary and log excerpt without access to other logs, reputation tools, endpoint telemetry, or real client confirmation.

## Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Fictional alerts documented | 10 |
| Model-generated alert reviews | 10 |
| Independent analyst verdicts | 2 |
| Broad verdict agreement (those 2) | 2 of 2 |
| Severity agreement (those 2) | 1 of 2 |
| Ground-truth validated malicious incidents | 0 |
| Live SOC investigations performed | 0 |

**These agreement counts are descriptive, not an accuracy score.** The sample is too small, purpose-built, and lacks independent ground truth.

## Important cases

**Alert 01 — repeated failed SSH login attempts:** Both the analyst and the AI classified the suspicious login-failure pattern as a true-positive detection with Medium severity. This does not show a successful SSH login or confirmed compromise.

**Alert 02 — success following repeated failures:** Both assessments marked the login To Be Confirmed. The analyst selected Medium severity; the AI recommended High because the deploy account successfully authenticated after repeated failures. The correct action depends on source authorization, deployment schedule, privileges, and post-login activity.

**Alert 03 — encoded PowerShell:** Word launched PowerShell with hidden and encoded switches, followed by an outbound connection attempt. That pattern should be investigated promptly, but the payload and network response were unavailable. The AI flagged suspicious detection, not proven compromise.

**Alerts 04–10:** The simulated set includes an internal scan, possible phishing, an approved scheduled task, an authorized antivirus EICAR test, unexplained periodic network traffic, authorized RDP, and a possible high-volume external transfer. See the [comparison table](COMPARISON.md) for each result.

## Strengths of AI triage

- Summarizes process, login, email and network indicators quickly.
- Helps generate investigation checklists and escalation recommendations.
- Highlights missing evidence such as authorization, decoded commands, session history, and transferred data.
- Supports consistent use of TP, FP and TBC when verdict definitions are kept clear.

## Limitations and failure risks

- **Unsupported certainty:** A match or anomaly cannot prove successful compromise, command-and-control, or data theft without supporting evidence.
- **Missing environment context:** Change tickets, account ownership, approved scanners and deployment schedules often determine whether activity is benign.
- **Risk of incorrect severity:** The Alert 02 severity disagreement shows how the same evidence can support different priorities.
- **False-positive interpretation:** A valid detection of an EICAR test or approved administrative event does not mean a real security incident.
- **Circular evaluation:** Reusing AI-written verdicts as purported independent human judgments would invalidate a Human vs AI comparison.
- **Privacy:** Client logs, personal information and credentials should not be pasted into external services without authorization.

## Recommendation

Use AI as a **supporting investigation tool**, not the final authority. Analysts should confirm source approval, examine complete log timelines and endpoint activity, follow the SOC's escalation procedures, and make the final decision based on verifiable evidence.
